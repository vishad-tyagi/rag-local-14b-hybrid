Title: Meridian Trust Bank Risk Operations Handbook
Department: Financial Crime, Credit Risk, and Customer Operations
Version: 2026-Q2

Section 1: Customer Risk Segmentation
Meridian Trust Bank assigns each customer a risk segment of Low, Medium, or High. Risk segmentation is based on onboarding information, transaction behavior, fraud alerts, and credit exposure. A customer should be reviewed manually if they are High risk and have either an unresolved AML alert or transaction activity above normal peer behavior.

High-risk customers require enhanced due diligence. Enhanced due diligence includes reviewing source of funds, recent transaction counterparties, account opening history, and any connected accounts or merchants. Analysts should document the reason for escalation before closing a review.

Section 2: AML Alert Handling
An AML alert is considered urgent when its alert score is 80 or higher. Urgent AML alerts must be reviewed within one business day. If an urgent alert is linked to a customer with a High risk segment, the case should be escalated to the Financial Crime Review Team.

Alerts with scores between 60 and 79 are moderate priority. Moderate-priority alerts should be reviewed within three business days. Alerts below 60 may be monitored unless they are connected to repeated suspicious transaction patterns.

A low-score AML alert can still require analyst attention when it is linked to repeated suspicious activity, unusual counterparties, restricted accounts, or transaction behavior that exceeds stated policy thresholds. Low score alone is not enough to close an alert when other risk signals are present.

Section 3: Suspicious Transaction Patterns
The bank treats repeated high-value transfers to the same merchant as a suspicious pattern when the total transferred amount exceeds 10000 USD within a short review window. Cash withdrawals above 5000 USD should be checked against the customer's historical activity. International transfers should receive additional review when the destination merchant or receiving party is not part of the customer's normal profile.

Section 4: Credit Risk Review
Loan accounts are monitored separately from deposit accounts. A loan is considered elevated credit exposure when the outstanding balance is above 50000 USD and the customer risk segment is Medium or High. Elevated exposure does not automatically mean fraud, but it should be considered when prioritizing manual reviews.

Section 5: Branch and Relationship Context
Branch context can help explain operational patterns. Customers associated with the Downtown branch often include business accounts with higher payment volume. Customers associated with the Airport branch may have more international transactions. Branch context should not override AML rules, but it can help analysts interpret whether activity is expected.

High business payment volume does not automatically mean suspicious activity. Business accounts may normally send larger or more frequent payments than individual accounts. Analysts should treat the activity as suspicious only when it also matches risk indicators such as repeated high-value transfers, urgent AML alerts, unusual counterparties, or threshold breaches.
